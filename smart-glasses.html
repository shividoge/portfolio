05  ·  The selling point

The same idea as a $799 pair, built for about a fifth of the price

Leading AR devices sell for $299–$799 and weigh 250–400 g, and consumer adoption is still below 15%. The gap is economic and ergonomic, not technological. S³ targets a $149 shelf price on about $83 of parts.

06  ·  What it does

Press a button, ask anything, read the answer on your lens

The glasses hear you, turn speech into text on the chip, and pass the question to a companion phone app over a hotspot. The phone asks Gemini and sends the reply back to the ESP32, which draws it on the OLED. A camera frame can ride along when the question needs one. Try it. The 3D model above reacts too.

07  ·  How we built it

Two decisions made it affordable and buildable

We scored every concept against weighted criteria before cutting any plastic. Cheap off-the-shelf electronics beat a custom board, and a compact arm-mounted projector beat a lens-mounted one.

Electronics weighted score, max 500

The ESP32 module wins on cost (30% weight), manufacturability and repairability. A custom PCB would be sleeker and hold a bigger battery, so it is the v2 plan.

Display weighted score, max 500

The compact projector keeps every optic inside the frame, out of your line of sight, at the price of some distortion risk from the lens.

08  ·  Results

Four clean passes, one partial, one miss

Every requirement had a pass line and a stated measurement tolerance before we tested. Mass, latency, LED and surface temperature passed. Battery life missed. Display visibility passed indoors and in shade, and failed in direct sun.

Performance vs requirements 5 trials each unless noted

Mass pass

86.66 g mean over five weighings on a calibrated scale, ±1 g. 42% under the 150 g cap.

Latency pass

Question sent to answer on display, 5 trials, mean 1,315 ms. The Gemini call is most of it.

Surface temperature pass

One hour under heavy load, IR thermometer at 0.95 emissivity. Peak 39.5 °C against a 43 °C skin-contact limit.

Battery life miss

200 mAh cell. Continuous use 1.4 h, idle 11.3 h. Heavy (50% active) and moderate (12% active) are weighted harmonic means of those two measurements. Moderate use gives 6.1 h against an 8 h goal.

Display visibility partial

Legible in light and dark rooms and in shade. Washed out in direct sunlight, the same feedback our peer reviewers gave: very visible at night, needs daytime brightness work.

Recording LED 5 / 5

The RGB LED lit clearly whenever the camera or mic was recording, every trial. That is what 18 U.S.C. §2511 asks for.

Limits & what's next

What S³ is not, and the v2 plan

Known limits

  • Battery. 6.1 h moderate use against an 8 h goal, limited by the 200 mAh cell.
  • Sunlight. The OLED cannot beat direct sun yet.
  • Small display. Monochrome 64×32 OLED. It is a heads-up display, not a screen floating in the room.
  • Untested. Water and impact resistance (IP54 target) were skipped so we would not destroy the only prototype.
  • Needs a phone. The hotspot and companion app do the cloud work.

v2 plan

  • Custom PCB for even weight and a 350 mAh battery in the same space.
  • Brightness and adaptive display to fix daylight legibility.
  • Better lens alignment to reduce optical distortion.
  • Market test. Cost modelling and user testing at volume.

Built to last

  • Modular. Battery, display and ESP32 each replace on their own. The OLED failed mid-build and the modular design saved it.
  • Recyclable. PETG, off-the-shelf parts.
  • Safe. LED for recording, brightness tuned to IEC 62471, skin surfaces under 43 °C.
  • Looks normal. Standard eyeglass shape, which cuts the stigma of wearing AR.
